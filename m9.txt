fix(net): stage 5 - retries counts attempts, not loss evidence: a healthy reliable stream was reset after 200 ms of receiver silence

Chromium failed two browser witnesses on the Linux runner while
Firefox passed all 26 and Windows passed twice. The tempting reading
was a sampling window in the witnesses. It was not: the witnesses
were right, their waits were already count-based with a 30 s ceiling,
and the defect is in production.

**Reproduced, not inferred.** Three injections settle it. Losing one
real datagram native to leaf is RECOVERED here - the retransmit
arrives and all 26 witnesses pass, so a 30 s ceiling is about a
hundred times the recovery latency and cannot be the problem. Losing
a payload and every retransmit reproduces the CI SHAPE but leaves
`stream_failed: 1`, which CI did not show. What reproduces CI exactly
is blocking the tab's main thread for 1.2 s across the burst: every
payload delivered and acknowledged, `duplicate_sequence` at 36 - three
spurious retransmits times twelve packets, an RTO storm on packets
that were never lost - and the anchor declaring the stream failed
anyway. Zero data loss, and a healthy stream destroyed by receiver
slowness.

**The mechanism.** `retries` counts retransmit ATTEMPTS rather than
evidence that anything was lost, and the RTO never backed off. At
`DEFAULT_RTO` 50 ms, a 25 ms tick and 3 retries, the entire budget
expired about 200 ms after a packet went unacked. A receiver that was
merely LATE - a starved browser tab on a loaded runner - therefore
cost its sender the stream: failed, `StreamReset` sent, the receiver
retiring its receive half, and every later payload on that stream
silently dropped. The give-up was silent too, which is why this
needed a reproduction rather than a read.

**The fix** is both halves, because neither alone suffices: RFC 6298
doubling per attempt capped at `MAX_RTO` (the first retransmit stays
prompt at one RTO; only repeated failure stretches), and a retry
budget of 7 spanning about 5.15 s - twenty-five times the observed
stall and well inside the 30 s transfer timeout. Backoff alone gives
350 ms; a bigger count alone gives 350 ms plus a flood.
`give_up_horizon` publishes the ladder so layers above, and tests
that must wait for a real give-up, reason about pacing instead of
hardcoding a constant. Two dependents now wait the published horizon
rather than a magic sleep.

One test was deleted rather than re-pinned:
`test_reliable_stream_max_retries_exhausted` existed only to assert
"N attempts at a fixed cadence and out", and under backoff it would
have passed vacuously - the packet simply not yet due. The contract
it claimed to cover is covered properly by
`exhausted_retransmits_flag_failure_and_drop`.

**The witnesses gain evidence, not tolerance.** Every assertion about
what must arrive is unchanged. The failing row now prints the leaf's
counters, `waited_ms`, whether the iterator ended, and the ANCHOR's
reliable-stream ledger with a one-line reading - gave up, still owned,
or acknowledged but undelivered - because the CI row that lost a
payload printed no counters at all. Leg 3 rides its own stream, so a
short earlier leg can no longer hide this leg's payload in a skipped
prefix, and the outcome classifier now says what was observed instead
of announcing a surplus in a round where nothing arrived at all.
